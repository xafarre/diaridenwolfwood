I wake in the hospital. Khor is there too. Wúsir greets us in his usual manner, as though his pleasure makes him repeat himself.

“You’re awake! I’m glad. Very glad.”

I let him talk. After the dome, being able to listen to him is reassurance enough for now.

“You were in a dreadful state, both of you. But I’ve managed to heal your wounds.”

Toni appears and joins in the relief with a familiarity I still find grating.

“Well, lads! You’re awake. I’m glad, truly. But what possessed you to go so far inside?”

I dislike his tone, but he came back when we needed him. I owe him thanks for that, however hard it is to reconcile with everything else I think of him.

“Thank you for bringing reinforcements.”

“It was nothing, nothing. Samón is waiting for you.”

The relief of waking lasts until we find ourselves before Samón. He is angry and makes no effort to hide it.

“You disobeyed orders. Let that be the last time.”

He does not seem disappointed. He is angry, but I want to see relief as well: we are standing before him, and he knows we were trying to do something good. He does not spare us the reprimand.

“You went into a dangerous place without equipment. You still haven’t got what you need. The supply shipment will arrive soon; we’ll give you an advance on your pay so you can equip yourselves.”

For now, we have to wait for the train. Samón bends a few slats of the metal blind and shows us a room full of people. We recognise faces from Ballard; now he explains what each of them does.

“Kuma and Kuna. They handle special matters, special operations. Kuma is the officer, the older one. She’s in charge of the pair.”

Then he points out a man in a grey suit with slicked-back hair.

“Franco. The escorts handle what happens outside Suelex.”

I see gold on one hand: one ring, perhaps two. I find him neither handsome nor ugly. Around him, the men in black dinner suits are meticulously clean-shaven, their hair slicked back as well. I listen to the names, trying to match them to the faces.

“Gabalin is responsible for perimeter security.”

The man attached to the machine is there too. He now has triangular tracks like a tank’s. We have seen him with two legs. I do not yet know how much of his body can change without changing the man above it.

Toni takes us outside and into the woods, to a spot among rocks that seems chosen so nobody will reach it by chance.

“This is Javi, from maintenance. And el Chino, from logistics.”

El Chino does not look particularly interested in making friends. There is a cask of spirits on the ground, and we start drinking. Toni has already told them what he saw us do; from their reception, I get the impression he spoke well of us. Now he wants the part he missed.

“Go on, tell us. What did you find after we split up?”

We tell him about the stairs and the trail of blood, the open grilles in the walkway room, and the beasts waiting farther on. Javi and el Chino listen, occasionally exclaiming in surprise. Toni keeps returning to the details.

“They were waiting for you?”

“Some were high up on the wall. They seemed coordinated. Then we found one that wouldn’t move, even when we threw a stone at it.”

“It didn’t move?”

“It watched us. The others stood still too, before we went into the dome.”

Telling it all in sequence brings back that silence between attacks. We did not know then why they stood still. Here, with spirits to drink and people listening, we have no explanation either.

We go on to the glass dome, the columns of hardened mud and the creature perched on them. We describe the high-pitched sound it made with its wings. Toni listens with an interest that is not satisfied merely to know we survived.

“And that beast? How did you kill it?”

“Khor tore its body open with his claws.”

So far, I have not lied. After the blow, the beast fled towards the back of the room. What came next is the part I do not want to explain.

“I finished it off,” Khor says.

Gebedayah backs the same account. I have not had to ask either of them. We keep talking, and the shot stays out of the story, as though nothing else happened between Khor’s claws and the appearance of the second beast. With so much attention on us, I am grateful they both understood what I needed to leave unsaid.

The conversation turns to where those creatures come from. We are told they emerge from the Void: extremely strange, exceptional, unclassified things. I listen without any way to verify the explanation. We now have a name for the place they supposedly come from; we still do not know what else we may find inside the mine.

Toni moves easily among these people. He knows them, talks to them, and has brought us here to meet them. The manner that tires me also opens doors for him. I take a moment to speak into his ear.

“Do you have vigilia?”

The question surprises him, but he does not seem alarmed.

“Well now… I wasn’t expecting that from you.”

I wait for an answer. I did not ask what he expected of me.

“When the shipment arrives, you might be able to get some.”

He stops short of a promise. He lets me understand that there will be a way, and with that the conversation can return to the others. Samón spoke of the same shipment as a means of equipping us; now I also hope it brings what I have had to ask for in a whisper.

We keep drinking. Khor remains reluctant to talk to Toni throughout the gathering. Even coming back with reinforcements seems not to have changed his opinion. I do not find Toni any less tiresome either, but I have thanked him and just asked him a favour. When the train arrives, it will be in my interest to speak to him again.
