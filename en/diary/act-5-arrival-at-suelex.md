We leave Ballard as the day draws to a close. Two days on the train await us, with a newly formed team and a leader I would not have chosen.

During the journey we see a water elemental. Everyone is surprised, and I take the opportunity to explain how we understand such manifestations in the Order of Electra.

“We believe they arise from natural imbalances. With electrical elementals, it can happen during a great storm or after an accident in a Tesla installation. The current finds nowhere to flow, takes on a form with a will of its own, and waits to be released, redirected.”

That is what the Order teaches; the water creature alone gives me no way to test it.

The train enters a valley with houses climbing both slopes. Patches of green remain amid the dust and heat, but haze blurs the outlines of the more distant buildings. Suelex looks abandoned rather than destroyed: the houses still stand, surrounded by metal structures that make it clear what sustained the town.

As soon as we stop, Toni is already calling us out.

“Come on, everyone off!”

Khor waits until last to leave. The station stands at the edge of town, near the cliff. We step onto a platform of metal and asphalt where the railway ends. Beyond it, separate mine-cart rails begin, leading onto a bridge wide enough to leave room to walk on either side.

Where the map marks the mine, a structure of iron, steel, copper and timber rises out of the canyon. It could be sixteen hundred metres tall, but its lower reaches vanish into the mist, and I struggle to judge the distance. Pipes, floors, chains and an enormous turbine fill that height. Above them, buildings pile upon one another into something like an industrial Gothic castle. Sections jut out, stretches of metal framework remain unfinished, and a tower with an immense clock stands beside a glass dome attached to one flank.

The main bridge leads there from the town. Farther down, three narrow bridges leave the structure at different heights and enter the cliff faces, where the mine lifts are accessed. None reaches the top of the rock. From here, the lowest crossings begin to merge with the dust hanging in the canyon.

We are sent to check that the building is safe. I cross the bridge trembling, more conscious of the drop than of the rails beside me. The wind and the noises of the structure send shivers through me despite the heat. I stop to look down: the rock walls fade before I can make out the bottom. The clock reads nine forty and does not move. At the centre of the structure I can make out a lift suspended from enormous chains.

As we enter the building, we hear human and animal cries among the dust and furniture. At the far end, another security team is fighting creatures a metre tall, like rabid dogs raised onto two legs. They have no fur. Skin wrinkles over their bodies, their tails are short and thick, and their mouths hold too many teeth.

I count a dozen around the other team, but with so much movement I cannot tell how many more are hidden in the room. I pick up a heavy gear from the floor and throw it at one creature’s head. It falls. Khor and Gebedayah join the fight; soon each of us has more than six of the animals coming at him in a fury as we try to force them back.

The other team is struggling. I see little help from Mònica; none from Toni. As we bring creatures down, the survivors take fright and flee upstairs.

Someone calls us over: a young woman is wounded. I approach, find the injury in her side and place my left palm over it. I deploy the four rods. Only the short terminals remain visible on the back of my hand, linked by electrical arcs; smoke escapes between my fingers. When I lift my hand, the wound has closed.

Her companions tell us about another young woman who has disappeared.

“They took her upstairs. The animals dragged her away.”

I look at the stairs. The creatures are ferocious, but I struggle to credit them with the strength to carry off a person. The gap between what I have seen and what I am being told leaves me uneasy.
